A quick primer before you dive into the atlas below — no biology background needed.

🧬
What's a gene?

A gene is a short stretch of DNA that acts like an instruction for building one specific protein. Proteins are the working parts of a cell — some send signals, some repair damage, some control when a cell divides.

🔤
What are the letters?

DNA is written using just 4 "letters" — A, T, G, C — each standing for a different molecule (adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine). The order they're strung in is what encodes the instructions.

⚠️
What's a mutation?

A mutation is just a typo in that sequence — one letter swapped for another. Most are harmless. But if a typo lands in exactly the wrong spot, it can change the protein enough to break how the cell behaves.

⚖️
Oncogene vs. tumor suppressor

Think of a cell like a car. Oncogenes are like the gas pedal — a mutation can jam it down, so the cell divides nonstop. Tumor suppressors are like the brakes — a mutation can disable them, so nothing stops runaway growth.

Apoptosis Evasion Hallmark of cancer
▶

Normal cells are built to kill themselves when something goes wrong — DNA damage, a missing growth signal, a failed division. This built-in self-destruct program is called apoptosis. Cancer cells survive by disabling it, letting damaged cells that should have died keep dividing instead.

Damaged cell detects the problem, triggers apoptosis
→
Cancer cell blocks the death signal, keeps dividing
Normally

Stress or DNA damage activates p53, which switches on pro-death signals (like BAX) that punch holes in the mitochondria, releasing cytochrome c and triggering caspases that dismantle the cell.

How cancer evades it

Tumor suppressors like TP53 get mutated and stop working, or anti-apoptotic proteins like BCL-2 are overproduced and physically block the death signal from ever reaching the mitochondria.

Why it matters for treatment

Many cancer drugs (including some CRISPR and small-molecule strategies) work by forcing this pathway back open — restoring apoptosis rather than just stopping growth.
